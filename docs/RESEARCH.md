# Research behind Behind the Glass

## The problem
- Fundraising is a numbers game founders can't rehearse: Techstars says most founders need 100–200 conversations to close a pre-seed or seed round, and Carta saw 11 checks written from ~4,500 pitch decks (0.2%). Every bad pitch burns a real investor.
- Founders lose most on terms they don't understand. SVB: first-time founders often don't grasp liquidation preferences and obsess over valuation while missing other provisions. YC: loss of board control is the most significant risk because founders can be fired from their own company. Carta and Contrary flag >1x and participating preferences as red flags.
- Existing AI pitch tools (Kydarin, PitchPulse, Yoodli, Fi.co VC practice, VC Mirror, DemoDay AI, "Shark Tonk") are single-AI pitch scorers. None show investors deliberating privately, none run the term sheet negotiation, none price the outcome in dollars.

## How real partner meetings work (basis for the cast)
- First Round Review: a first meeting is usually with one partner; the partner meeting can include 5–15 investors depending on the firm.
- The sponsoring ("point") partner presents the deal, the founder pitches (15–30 min), the partnership asks questions (30–60 min), then the founder leaves and the partners discuss and decide.
- Associates or principals may attend and take notes; USV's Monday meeting includes partners, analysts and general counsel.

## Benchmarks used in the scorecard (`bruin/assets/carta_benchmarks.csv`)
- 2026 median seed valuation ~$19.7M through Q3 (Carta State of Private Markets Q3 2026).
- Seed / Series A median dilution 19–20% (Carta 2025 in review).
- Clean terms: 1x non-participating preference (YC standard term sheet).
